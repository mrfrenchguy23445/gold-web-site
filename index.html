What it is

Built from scratch by our amazing developer team.

Gold-Web is its own browser, not a reskinned version of Chrome or a modified copy of Firefox. It uses the Chromium Embedded Framework (CEF) as its rendering engine, with the rest of the browser built around it.

Chromium’s engine, without Chrome’s habits

Websites use Chromium’s rendering engine, including WebGL and WebAssembly content such as Eaglercraft. The browser interface is separate from that engine, so Gold-Web can provide its own features without bringing along Chrome’s sponsored tiles or browser interface.

Our interface, our policy

We build the window, tabs, address bar, menus and built-in pages ourselves. The browser uses a C++ shell with a web-based interface, while privacy-related behaviour is handled separately from the interface.

Yours to restyle

Colours, corner styles and spacing are controlled by design tokens. Gold-Web includes twelve presets, and you can switch between them or create your own without rewriting the interface.

Privacy

Privacy, stated honestly.

We want the privacy information to be clear about what the browser actually does. Here is what is already in place, along with the limits of the current protection.

Blocks known trackers. Not “makes you anonymous”.

True today

  • Tracker and ad blocking currently use a hand-curated host list. It is an early version and does not yet offer per-site controls.
  • Gold-Web does not add account services, sync or usage reports, and it does not send that information elsewhere.
  • No sponsored tiles and no “recommended” anything.
  • Browsing history is stored locally on your device, alongside the browser profile.

What it doesn’t claim

  • Gold-Web does not make you anonymous or route your traffic through another network. It can block known trackers, but a website may still recognise you through information it already has.
  • It won’t defeat a determined fingerprint.
  • The blocking system relies on a curated list, so it will not catch every tracker.
  • The current version applies its curated list to subresources and does not yet include per-site controls.

Gold UI

Black surface, one gold accent, used sparingly.

The default theme keeps things simple, using black surfaces with gold reserved for important details and interactive elements. There are no gradients, glass effects or decorative shadows competing for attention.

What you can change

  • Preset: twelve named themes, from Gold to Daylight.
  • Accent: any colour. The rest of the scale is derived from it, and contrast is computed rather than hardcoded.
  • Tone: how black the surfaces are.
  • Corners, density, motion and transparency, from square to round, compact to spacious, full to off.
  • Background: solid, aura, mesh, grid or image, tinted from your accent.

Why it holds together

Components use the same design tokens rather than defining their own colours and spacing. When you choose a different theme, each part of the interface reads the updated values, helping the layout stay consistent.

Status

Where it is today.

Gold-Web is still in its early stages, with Linux as the first supported platform. The list below separates features that are available now from the work planned for later.

Working today

  • A frameless window with our own title bar and window buttons.
  • Tabs, each backed by its own real browser: open, close, switch and drag to reorder.
  • The tab strip can sit on the top, left or right edge.
  • Back, forward, reload, stop and search from the address bar.
  • Our own new-tab and home pages, with no feeds and no sponsored tiles.
  • Keyboard shortcuts that work while a page has focus.
  • On-device history: a searchable list of every visit, clearable in one action.
  • Tracker blocking, first cut, with counts in the Protection Centre.
  • Every surface follows the theme, live.

Next

  • A per-site shield: see and change what’s blocked for one site.
  • Data controls: cookies, cache and site data, reviewed and cleared.
  • Encrypted DNS with a named resolver you can choose, and an HTTPS-only mode.
  • A page that shows exactly what Gold-Web itself connects to, and CEF’s own background services switched off.
  • A fingerprinting self-test that explains what it changes and what it can’t.
  • Workspaces, bookmarks, downloads and passwords.
  • Linux packages (.deb, AppImage, Flatpak) and signed updates.
  • Windows, via the same CEF engine.

Download

Get Gold-Web for your system.

Gold-Web is in early development. We detect your system and show what is actually available for it, so you are never sent to a download that doesn’t exist.

    Build it

    Four commands from a clean checkout.

    To build Gold-Web, you will need a C++ toolchain, CMake, Ninja and the CEF binary distribution used by the project’s fetch script.

    $ ./scripts/fetch-cef.sh
$ cmake -S app -B build -G Ninja -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
$ cmake --build build
$ ./build/goldweb

    Gold-Web currently builds on Linux. Windows and macOS are planned for v1.0. The full setup instructions are in docs/BUILDING_LINUX.md.
